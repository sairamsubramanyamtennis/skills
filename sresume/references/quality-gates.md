# Quality score, red flags and the final gates

SKILL.md §12 is the compact version.

## The 0–100 diagnostic score

Diagnostic, not scientific. Its purpose is to **prioritize work**, so always report the three
weakest dimensions and the specific fix for each — a bare number is useless to the candidate.

| Dimension | Pts | Scoring at full marks |
|---|--:|---|
| Positioning | 12 | One professional identity is unmistakable in the top third of page one |
| Seniority signaling | 10 | Verb register, scope language and space allocation all match the target rung |
| Technical depth | 10 | Systems are described with enough substance that an engineer believes them |
| Accomplishment strength | 10 | Impact dominates responsibility; the best work is in the top three bullets |
| Quantification | 8 | Numbers where they exist, anchored, honestly attributed, none invented |
| Relevance | 8 | Space is allocated by relevance to the target, not by chronology |
| ATS compatibility | 8 | Parses cleanly, standard headings, terminology discoverable, no stuffing |
| Recruiter readability | 8 | Who/level/expertise/domain/technologies clear in ~15 seconds |
| Career narrative | 8 | Trajectory is visible; promotions are not hidden; jumps have context |
| Formatting | 6 | Typography, spacing, alignment, page breaks all professional |
| Differentiation | 6 | Passes the stranger test |
| Human authenticity | 6 | Passes the Humanization Pass and the "would I say this" test |

**Bands.** 90+ ready to send · 75–89 strong, specific fixes remain · 60–74 real problems, worth a
rebuild of the weak sections · under 60 the positioning is probably wrong, not the writing.

Score the resume *against its target*. The same document can be an 88 for a principal architect
role and a 55 for a VP Engineering role. Always say what you scored it against.

## Red flags

Identify these explicitly, and explain **why each matters** — a flagged problem the candidate does
not understand is a problem they will reintroduce.

**Structural**
- Excessive length for the level
- Dense unbroken text; no white space
- Weak page-one positioning — the top third wasted on a generic summary
- Repeated accomplishments across roles
- Obsolete technologies dominating the space

**Content**
- Generic summary that would fit anyone
- Responsibility-heavy bullets ("responsible for", "duties included")
- Missing metrics where they clearly exist
- Skills listed with no supporting evidence anywhere in the experience
- Claims inconsistent with the rest of the experience
- Excessive technical detail for an executive target
- Managerial language for an IC target — and the reverse

**Credibility**
- Keyword stuffing, or a skills section that is a wall
- Career gaps with no context
- Unclear promotions — several years, one title, no ladder
- Confusing or non-standard titles left unexplained
- Seniority claims that the described scope does not support
- AI experience whose production-vs-experimental status is deliberately ambiguous

Distinguish a *fatal* flag (fabrication, an unexplainable claim, wrong-level positioning) from a
*cosmetic* one and say which is which. Candidates fix what they are told is urgent.

## The final quality gate

Every item, before delivery. Do not skip because the draft feels good — a good-feeling draft is
exactly when the authenticity checks fail.

| Gate | Check |
|---|---|
| **Accuracy** | No fabricated technology, certification, degree, title, team size, figure, project, clearance, patent or publication |
| **Positioning** | The target professional identity is obvious without hunting |
| **Seniority** | Language matches the target level |
| **Technical credibility** | Every term used accurately; nothing chosen because it sounds impressive |
| **Evidence** | Every important claim has supporting experience behind it |
| **Impact** | Accomplishments outweigh generic responsibilities |
| **ATS** | Relevant terminology discoverable; parses cleanly; standard headings |
| **Recruiter readability** | The top of page one answers who/level/expertise/domain/differentiator |
| **Formatting** | Typography and spacing professional; no orphaned heading, no bullet split across pages |
| **Consistency** | Dates, titles, skills and technologies agree with each other and with LinkedIn |
| **Interview defensibility** | Every important claim survives ten minutes of a Principal Engineer's questions |
| **Human authenticity** | Sounds like this particular candidate |
| **JD independence** | Aligned with the role without appearing copied from the posting |
| **Personal voice** | The candidate's career identity is still visible |

## Final human authenticity review

Twelve questions. Any concern means revise before delivering.

1. Does this sound like this particular candidate?
2. Is there enough specific evidence to feel lived rather than generated?
3. Are the technical statements credible to an engineer?
4. Are there unnecessary buzzwords?
5. Is the language excessively polished or promotional?
6. Are sentence structures unnaturally repetitive?
7. Did we mirror the job description too closely?
8. Could the candidate comfortably explain every statement?
9. Would a skeptical recruiter perceive a sincere applicant?
10. Does it contain memorable details specific to this person's career?
11. Would the candidate recognize themselves in it?
12. Does it sound confident without trying too hard to impress?

## What to report

With a score, always give: the number, what it was scored against, the three weakest dimensions
with a concrete fix each, any fatal red flag separately from cosmetic ones, and the single change
that would improve the resume most. One prioritized change beats twelve suggestions.
