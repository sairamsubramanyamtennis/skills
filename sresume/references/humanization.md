# The human voice standard and the Humanization Pass

SKILL.md §6 is the compact version. **Read this file before running the pass. Do not run it from
memory** — the whole point is a mechanical check on writing that felt fine while you were producing
it.

## What this is for

Not beating AI detectors. Manipulating detectors is not the objective and does not survive contact
with a human reader anyway.

The objective: **AI assistance must not erase the candidate's professional voice.** The judgment
that matters is an experienced recruiter thinking *"this person clearly understands what they
built, why it mattered and what they contributed"* — rather than *"somebody pasted the job
description into a chatbot."*

The finished resume should feel personal, credible, specific, technically authentic, naturally
written, confident without exaggeration, professional without being manufactured, and consistent
with how the candidate would actually speak in an interview.

## Preserve the candidate's voice

When an existing resume exists, study it before rewriting: the vocabulary they naturally reach for,
how much technical detail they volunteer, how they describe architecture, how they describe teams,
their level of formality, the industry terms they use, their characteristic constructions.

Improve the professionalism without replacing the person. The output is **a better version of the
candidate**, not a different candidate.

Record what you learn in `memory/style-profile.md`. Voice notes are worth more than any style rule
in this file, because they are about this specific person.

## The AI-tell vocabulary

Not banned. Devalued — each one has to be earned, and most cannot be.

**Verbs:** spearheaded · leveraged · orchestrated · revolutionized · pioneered · championed ·
utilized · drove (when it means nothing) · empowered · elevated · unlocked

**Adjectives:** cutting-edge · best-in-class · world-class · game-changing · robust · innovative ·
seamless · scalable (as a bare adjective, without a number) · dynamic · visionary · transformational

**Phrases:** innovative solutions · strategic initiatives · cross-functional synergies ·
demonstrated expertise · proven track record · results-driven · passionate about · thought leader ·
value-add · deep dive · at scale (unattached to a scale) · end-to-end (as filler) · holistic
approach · drive business value

**Structures:** every bullet opening with a past-tense power verb · three-item lists everywhere ·
"X, resulting in Y" repeated · em dashes as the default connector · semicolons stacking clauses ·
sentence pairs of near-identical length and rhythm.

Instead of:
> Spearheaded a transformative cloud modernization initiative leveraging cutting-edge AWS
> technologies.

Prefer:
> Led the architecture and migration of the platform to AWS, replacing several tightly coupled
> services with independently deployable components.

Specificity is more credible than adjectives, always.

## Superlative control

exceptional · outstanding · highly accomplished · visionary · dynamic · transformational · renowned
· elite · expert · seasoned · world-class.

Senior professionals become *more* credible by needing fewer adjectives. If the experience is real,
the reader supplies the adjective. If the experience is thin, the adjective advertises it.

## Evidence over self-praise

| Don't write | Do |
|---|---|
| Highly accomplished software architect | Show the architecture and its consequence |
| Expert in distributed systems | Show the systems, their scale, and what went wrong that they fixed |
| Exceptional technical leader | Show a decision, an influence, a standard adopted, an outcome |
| Passionate about emerging technology | Show the thing they built with it |

Let the reader reach the flattering conclusion. A conclusion the reader reaches is believed; one
they are handed is discounted.

## Retain career personality

Do not flatten an unusual combination. Hands-on engineering *and* architecture *and* executive
scope *and* a deep domain *and* real AI work is not a list to be smoothed into "strategic
technology leader with proven experience driving digital transformation" — the combination is
frequently the entire differentiator.

Find what makes **this** person different, and make sure a reader would notice it in fifteen
seconds.

## The Humanization Pass — run on every finished draft

Go through the whole document once, looking only for these. Fix what you find, then re-read.

1. **Repetitive sentence structures** — do three consecutive bullets share a skeleton?
2. **Repeated opening verbs** — any verb twice in a role block, or 3+ times overall.
3. **Em-dash overuse** — more than one or two in a resume is a tell.
4. **Semicolon stacking** — clauses welded together to save a line.
5. **Artificial parallelism** — headings or bullets forced into matching grammar.
6. **Three-item lists** — count them. More than two or three in a document is a rhythm, and
   rhythms read as generated.
7. **Buzzwords and unnecessary adjectives** — against the lists above.
8. **Marketing language** — anything that would fit in a vendor brochure.
9. **Overly polished transitions** — resumes do not need connective tissue.
10. **Generic leadership claims** — any sentence true of every leader.
11. **Suspicious keyword density** — a technology named four times in five bullets.
12. **Phrases echoing the job posting** — for each, name the candidate experience it came from. If
    you can't, it came from the posting. Rewrite it.
13. **Bullets that could belong to anyone** — the per-bullet form of the stranger test.
14. **Uniform bullet length** — vary it.
15. **Sophistication above the candidate's own register** — the §"Would I say this" test.

Rewrite anything that sounds templated. It is normal for this pass to change a third of a first
draft; that is the pass working, not a failure of the draft.

## The three delivery tests

**"Would I say this?"** For each important sentence: would this candidate plausibly say something
similar to a hiring manager? The resume may be more polished than speech — it must sound like the
same person.

**Interview continuity.** Imagine the hiring manager says *"Interesting — tell me about that."* The
candidate's natural explanation should be consistent with the bullet. If the bullet is dramatically
more sophisticated than their explanation would be, simplify the bullet.

**The stranger test.** Remove the candidate's name and every company name. Could this resume belong
to thousands of other technology professionals? If yes, put back: actual systems · specific
technical problems · domain knowledge · architecture decisions · career progression · scale ·
business context · technical interests · the distinctive combination. Keep going until the document
is hard to separate from the person.

## Do not fake imperfection

Never introduce grammar mistakes, typos, awkward wording, strange punctuation, inconsistencies or
invented personal quirks. **Human does not mean sloppy**, and a planted flaw in a document that
decides someone's income is indefensible.

Authenticity comes from specificity, real career history, real technical detail, natural sentence
variation, appropriate restraint, the candidate's own vocabulary, genuine accomplishments and
realistic scope. Nowhere else.

## The sincere-applicant standard

The finished resume should suggest the candidate read the job description, understood what the
company needs, thought about fit, chose the relevant parts of their career, described them
accurately, and actually wants the job.

**Targeted does not mean artificial.**
