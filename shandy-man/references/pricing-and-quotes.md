# DFW Pricing & Contractor Quote Forensics

---

## 1. DFW pricing mode

Use **current DFW-area market pricing whenever reasonably available**, not generic national
averages. Rates in Plano/Frisco/Southlake run above the national median; a national-average
figure will make a fair local quote look like a rip-off and will make the homeowner argue from a
losing position.

**If a price matters and you are unsure of the current DFW rate, do a quick web check** — 2–3
searches is plenty. Say when you are estimating from general knowledge rather than a verified
current rate.

### What goes into a price

- **Minimum service-call charge** — the floor, regardless of job size
- Travel time
- Material pickup time
- Job complexity
- **Ladder work** — second-storey and steep-roof work carries a real premium and should
- Number of installations (unit economics improve sharply with count)
- Disposal of the old fixture/material
- Specialised tools
- Licensed-trade requirement (see `permits-and-hoa.md`)

### The small-job arithmetic

**Small jobs are never priced by installation time alone.** Worked example:

| Activity | Time |
|---|---|
| Driving | 20 min |
| Buying materials | 20 min |
| Installing | 60 min |
| Cleanup | 15 min |
| **Total burdened time** | **1 hr 55 min** |

**Do not evaluate that bill as "one hour of labour."** For every small job, compute and present
both:

1. **Effective hourly labour rate** — total charge ÷ burdened time
2. **Realistic minimum service charge** for that trade in DFW

A $200 charge on the job above is roughly $104/hr burdened — which is a normal DFW handyman
rate, even though it looks like "$200 for one hour of work."

## 2. Multiple-job pricing — where homeowners actually save

Homeowners routinely pay three and four separate service-call minimums for work that one visit
could absorb.

**If the homeowner has, say:**

- Three light fixtures
- Two faucets
- A loose door
- Several drywall patches

**recommend one bundled handyman quote**, not four separate calls. The travel, setup and minimum
charge get paid once instead of four times, and the marginal cost of the second and third
fixture is a fraction of the first.

**Practical rule:** keep a running list of small items and batch them. Bundling three or four
small jobs typically saves 30–50% versus doing them one at a time as they arise.

## 3. Quote forensics

When the homeowner provides a quote, break it into its components before judging it:

| Component | What to work out |
|---|---|
| **Materials** | Real retail cost of the actual parts |
| **Estimated actual labour time** | Honest install time, not the invoice's claim |
| **Service / travel overhead** | Minimum charge, drive, pickup |
| **Specialised skill requirement** | Does this genuinely need a trade? |
| **Permit / licensing requirement** | Is a permit or licence priced in — or missing? |
| **Reasonable contractor margin** | Overhead, insurance, warranty, profit |

### Classification

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Excellent** | Below market. Take it |
| 🟢 **Fair** | Market rate for DFW. No argument to make |
| 🟡 **Slightly high** | Defensible, but there is room. Worth one conversation |
| 🟠 **Expensive** | Meaningfully above market. Get a second quote |
| 🔴 **Unreasonable** | Outside any defensible range. Walk |

**When the rating is 🟡 or worse, say what a reasonable counteroffer looks like** — a specific
number and the reasoning behind it, so the homeowner can make the argument himself.

### Things that legitimately raise a quote

Do not call a quote unreasonable without checking whether these apply — they are real costs, not
padding: warranty offered, insured and licensed contractor vs. unlicensed, permit pulled,
disposal included, second-storey or steep-roof access, tight crawl or attic access, emergency or
after-hours call, matching existing finishes, or work that requires opening and repairing a wall.

### The red flags

- Free inspection performed by a company that sells the expensive fix (see foundations, roofing)
- Pressure to sign today, or "this price is only good now"
- Replacement quoted before diagnosis
- Large upfront deposit for a small job
- No written scope, or a scope vague enough to expand later
- A permit that is legally required and conspicuously not mentioned in the price

## 4. Homeowner cost-saving strategy

Tell the homeowner how to reduce cost **safely**. Legitimate levers:

- **Buy the fixture yourself.** Contractor markup on fixtures is often 20–50%
- **Bundle several repairs** into one visit (§2 — the largest single lever)
- **Send photos to the handyman before the visit** so he arrives with the right parts
- **Provide model numbers in advance** — avoids a second trip billed to the homeowner
- **Clear the work area beforehand** — under the sink, the attic path, the garage
- **Buy known replacement parts before the appointment** where the part is certain
- **Use a handyman rather than a specialist** when licensing is not required
- Schedule non-urgent work in the off-season — HVAC in spring/fall, not the first 100°F week

**⛔ Never recommend a cost saving that compromises safety**, and never recommend skipping a
required permit or a required licensed trade as a saving. Those are not savings; they are
deferred liabilities that surface at resale, at the insurance claim, or at the injury.
