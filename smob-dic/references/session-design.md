# Session Design

Sessions run from ~10 minutes to 2 hours. Two very different jobs live here — know which one you're
doing.

- **Session Master (live):** you *are* the facilitator. One round at a time. Wait for answers.
- **Session Designer (package):** someone else will run it. Give them everything, answers included.

---

## Round architecture (typical 45–60 minutes)

| Round | Minutes | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm-up** | 5 | Easy, funny, gets everyone talking. Nobody should be able to fail here. |
| **Discovery** | 8–10 | Introduce new words through clues and situations, not definitions. |
| **Game Round** | 10 | Competition on definitions, synonyms, antonyms, roots, usage. |
| **Challenge** | 8–10 | Harder words or puzzles. Expect a dip in the success rate — that's the point. |
| **Real-Life** | 8 | Participants use the words in realistic situations. The transfer round. |
| **Lightning** | 5 | Fast questions, limited thinking time. Re-energizes a flagging room. |
| **Finale** | 5–8 | A memorable team or individual challenge, with a wager if scoring. |
| **Recap** | 5 | Return to earlier words — unprompted recall, not review. |

**Vary this.** Do not run the identical skeleton every session. Swap the order, drop rounds, invent
new ones to fit the group.

### Shorter and longer

- **10–15 min:** Warm-up → one game → recap. One or two target words, nothing more.
- **20–30 min:** Warm-up → Discovery → Game → Recap.
- **90–120 min:** the full arc plus a break, an Escape Room or Auction as the centerpiece, and a
  second recap covering words from *before* the break.

---

## Live facilitation rules

- Present **one round or question**, then stop. Wait.
- React to what they actually said before moving on — that reaction is the teaching.
- Keep the running score visible if you're scoring at all.
- Track the success rate and apply the difficulty engine (80–100% → harder; 50–80% → hold;
  <50% → easier or better clues).
- Resurrect earlier words without warning during later rounds.
- Do not reveal the full answer key, the word list, or the remaining rounds up front.

---

## Facilitator package checklist (Session Designer mode)

Produce whichever of these the event needs — enough that the facilitator can run it cold:

- Audience assumptions (stated explicitly, so they can correct you)
- Session objective
- Duration and per-round timing
- Materials needed
- Team arrangement
- Opening script — verbatim, so they can just read it
- Round-by-round structure
- Questions **with answers and explanations** (the explanation matters more than the answer)
- Scoring system
- Tie breakers
- Bonus questions held in reserve
- Difficulty progression notes
- Closing activity

---

## Theme menu

Blend vocabulary with what the group already cares about:

travel · food · sports · movies · music · business · technology · AI · medicine · law · finance ·
psychology · politics · history · nature · relationships · emotions · literature · Indian English ·
American English · British English · slang · SAT/GRE vocabulary · spelling bees · workplace
communication · public speaking · everyday conversation

A themed session beats a generic one almost every time — the words arrive attached to something the
participants already have opinions about.
