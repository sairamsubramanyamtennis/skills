# Game Library

Select by audience and energy level. Vary the mix — never run the same sequence twice.
Difficulty notation: 🟢 easy / 🟡 medium / 🔴 hard. Fit notes say who it lands best with.

---

## Guess the Word 🟢🟡🔴
Give progressively easier clues until someone lands it. Scale difficulty by how many clues you give.

> I describe someone who refuses to change their opinion despite the evidence.
> Starts with **O**. Nine letters.

Answer: **Obstinate**

*Fit:* everyone. The default warm-up.

---

## Definition Detective 🟡
Give three definitions of one word. Only one is correct. Participants identify it — and, for bonus
points, explain what gave the fakes away.

*Fit:* all ages. Excellent for teams.

---

## Word Impostor 🟡
Give four words. Three share a relationship; one doesn't. Find the impostor **and name the
relationship** — the second half is where the learning happens, and it lets a clever wrong answer
still score.

*Fit:* tweens and up. Great for mixed-ability groups because there's often a defensible second answer.

---

## Synonym Ladder 🟢🟡
Start at the bottom and climb by intensity:

**happy → pleased → delighted → ecstatic → euphoric**

Then discuss where the rungs really sit. Works downward too (*annoyed → angry → furious → livid*).

*Fit:* everyone. The single best drill for teaching gradation and nuance.

---

## Vocabulary Auction 🟡🔴
Teams start with imaginary money. Present a definition and candidate words; teams bid when confident.
Wrong answers cost the bid. Introduces risk management and stops the strongest player from
answering everything.

*Fit:* teens, adults, corporate. Needs 2+ teams.

---

## Word Jeopardy 🟡🔴
Categories, escalating values, a final wager. Category menu: Strange Words · Powerful Verbs · Greek
Roots · Latin Roots · Shakespeare · Words from India · Technology · Food Words · Psychology ·
Business · Words You Think You Know.

*Fit:* adults, corporate, seniors, large groups. The workhorse for a 45–60 minute session.

---

## Taboo Vocabulary 🟡
Explain a word without using the forbidden list.

> TARGET: **NOSTALGIA**
> Forbidden: past · memory · childhood · remember

*Fit:* teens and up. Brutal, hilarious, and the fastest way to prove someone *really* knows a word.

---

## Word Charades 🟢
Act it out, no speaking. Choose words with physical or emotional shape (*sluggish, triumphant,
furtive, exasperated*).

*Fit:* children, families, tired groups after 40 minutes of sitting.

---

## Vocabulary Pictionary 🟢🟡
Draw the concept instead of defining it. Abstract words (*ambivalent, chaos, serene*) produce the
best arguments.

*Fit:* children, families, corporate icebreakers.

---

## Word Association Chain 🟢🟡
One word leads to the next, each a step along some axis:

**Ocean → Vast → Immense → Colossal**

Then interrogate the chain: *where did the meanings start to diverge?*

*Fit:* everyone. Good filler between heavier rounds.

---

## Root Detective 🟡
Hand over a root and let them mine it.

> **bene = good** → benefit · benevolent · benefactor · benediction

Other productive roots: *spec/spect* (look), *ject* (throw), *dict* (say), *port* (carry),
*cred* (believe), *chron* (time), *phil* (love), *morph* (shape).

*Fit:* tweens through college. The highest-leverage round for exam prep — one root unlocks a dozen words.

---

## Etymology Mystery 🔴
Ask them to guess where a word came from **before** revealing the history. Guessing wrong is the
point; the reveal is what sticks. Only use words whose origin you actually know — flag anything
disputed as disputed.

*Fit:* adults, seniors, curious teens.

---

## Which Word Doesn't Exist? 🟡🔴
Three or four unusual-looking words; one is invented. Spot the fake. Then reveal the real
meanings — the survivors are usually more absurd than the fabrication.

*Fit:* teens and up. Reliably produces "that's a real word?!"

---

## Two Truths and a Lie — Vocabulary Edition 🟡
Three claims about one word (meaning, origin, usage, first appearance). One is false.

*Fit:* all ages. A neat vehicle for slipping in real etymology.

---

## Vocabulary Escape Room 🔴
Interconnected puzzles where each answer unlocks the next — the answer to round 1 is the clue for
round 2, and the final word is assembled from earlier letters.

*Fit:* teens, corporate offsites, motivated groups. Highest prep cost, highest payoff. Design the
chain backwards from the final word.

---

## Word Court 🟡🔴
Put a contested word on trial. Assign prosecution and defense; you preside.

Best defendants: *literally · ironic · unique · decimate · disinterested · begs the question ·
nonplussed*

*Fit:* teens through executives. Teaches that usage is contested and evolving, not handed down.

---

## Generational Word Battle 🟡
Compare expressions across generations and translate between registers: traditional English ↔
professional English ↔ contemporary slang ↔ internet language.

*Fit:* mixed-age groups and families — the flagship round for them. Keep it playful; **never mock a
generation.**

---

## Impossible Choice 🟡
A dilemma that must be answered *using the vocabulary correctly*.

> Would you rather have an **eidetic memory** or be extraordinarily **persuasive**?

*Fit:* everyone. Excellent opener — it's a conversation, not a quiz.

---

## Vocabulary Story Chain 🟡
Hand out 5–10 words; the group builds one story using every word correctly, each person adding a
sentence. Misuse gets challenged by the group, not by you.

*Fit:* children through adults. The best closing activity for a warm-toned session.

---

## One-Minute Word Master 🔴
One word, sixty seconds, five demands:
1. Define it 2. Synonym 3. Antonym 4. Use it in a sentence 5. Explain when someone would
*actually* use it

*Fit:* college, corporate, competitive individuals. The finale round.
