# Preparation plan templates — compressing intelligently

SKILL.md §7 is the compact version. Read this before building a plan.

**The compression rule: cut topics, never depth.** Under time pressure a shallow pass over ten topics
loses to defensible command of four, because interviews are won on follow-ups and follow-ups punish
shallowness. Equal-shortening is the instinct and it is wrong every time.

Ask for the real time budget — calendar days *and* hours he can actually give. Both. A two-week
runway with three available hours is a 3-hour plan with a long gap in it, not a two-week plan.

---

## 24 hours — emergency

**Teach nothing new.** New material at this range produces half-formed answers that break under the
first follow-up and cost more than the gap would have.

| Block | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1.5h | The three highest-probability questions, answered out loud, scored, redone |
| 1.5h | Story bank: the four stories this loop will probe. Numbers checked. Rehearsed aloud |
| 1h | One system-design round if the loop has one — for pacing and habit, not learning |
| 0.5h | Company and role: what this team is trying to do, and three questions to ask |
| 0.5h | Rapid-review sheet |
| — | The honest line for each known gap (`tutor-method.md` §5) |

Explicitly tell him what is **not** being fixed, so he is not surprised by it in the room.

---

## 3 days

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Gap analysis + risk register. Tutor the single worst Tier 1 gap. Retest |
| 2 | System design round + full attack + debrief. Story bank build-out |
| 3 | Mock interview in the actual round format. Score. Fix the top two defects. Rapid review |

At most **two** new topics. A third is where 3-day plans start failing.

---

## 1 week

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Engagement setup, competency map, gap analysis, risk register, plan |
| 2 | Tier 1 gap #1: teach → test → retest |
| 3 | Tier 1 gap #2: teach → test → retest. Cold retest of #1 |
| 4 | System design round + attack + debrief |
| 5 | Story bank: elicit, structure, rehearse. Metrics confirmed |
| 6 | Mock in the real format + persona. Score. Fix defects |
| 7 | Red team. Readiness. Rapid review |

Three to four new topics maximum. Day 3's cold retest of Day 2's material is not optional — it is
the only measurement of retention in the plan.

---

## 2 weeks

Week 1: map, gaps, and teaching — four to six topics, each with a retest, plus the first design
round mid-week to establish a baseline.

Week 2: practice-dominant — two design rounds, two mocks in different personas, story bank complete
with metrics, red team, readiness, rapid review. **Reserve the last two days entirely for practice
and review**; new material in the final 48 hours has a poor record.

Cold-retest everything from week 1 during week 2. Anything that fails a cold retest goes back on the
plan ahead of new material.

---

## 30 days

The only range where an **experience gap** can be partly addressed rather than only framed: he can
build something small and real, which converts "I understand it" into "I built a version of it and
here is what surprised me." That is a materially different answer.

- **Week 1** — map, gaps, plan. Teach the two hardest Tier 1 topics properly.
- **Week 2** — remaining Tier 1 + Tier 2. First design round. Begin the build, if one is warranted.
- **Week 3** — design rounds in two different problem shapes. Story bank complete. Full-loop mock.
- **Week 4** — red team, weak-area repair, second full-loop mock, readiness, rapid review.

Cold-retest weekly. On a 30-day plan the risk is not insufficient coverage; it is drift — steady
effort with no measurement. The tracker deltas are the measurement.

---

## Longer than 30 days, or no date

Switch modes. Run a standing competency-development plan against his *target level* rather than a
specific loop: one topic a week, taught and retested, plus a monthly design round and a quarterly
full-loop mock. The story bank gets built once and reused. When a real interview appears, the
engagement-specific plan starts from an already-high baseline and only has to cover the delta.

Say clearly which mode you are in. Standing development and interview prep have different
priorities, and conflating them wastes both.

---

## What to sacrifice, in order

1. Tier 3 entirely.
2. Breadth within Tier 2 — pick the two that this panel is most likely to probe.
3. Rehearsal of STRONG MATCH areas — down to one pass each, never zero (§7.2).
4. Second design round.
5. Company research beyond what supports three good questions.

**Never sacrifice:** the failure story · the top three predicted questions · one full round in the
real format · the rapid-review sheet.
